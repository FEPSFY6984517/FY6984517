Abstract

  This project investigates how advancements in engineering and material sciences have influenced and improved the designing and work 
  behind modern running shoe to benefit in performances. The three technological methods being; carbon fibre plates, advanced midsole 
  foam and smart materials in the upper are common advanced implementations into models. Where research has been done on each individual 
  component in detail on how they contribute to lowering energy costs through energy stores and transfers. Investigations on the Nike 
  Vapourfly 4% have been used to provide evidence for the methods and how they have impacted performance. Disadvantages of this development
  include material costs and durability concerns where sky high retail prices cannot justify the product for the customer. Throughout 
  innovation and development, the modern running shoe has proved to be developed though must be balanced with sustainability.

Introduction

  Running shoes hold a historic reputation in its constant development ability and performance. Over successive decades with advancements 
  in technology and material design we have seen running shoes originate from traditional leather sole to 3d printed, carbon plate models. 
  Through the growth in standards of competitive running, the manufacturing market for running shoes has had to closely adapt to stay ahead 
  of the game and competitors. Traditional running attire would be using synthetic textiles on the body of the shoe complete with a foam or 
  spikes set whereas now we are seeing new technologies being implemented such as carbon plating. These rely on three physical principles 
  within material science being; impact force, energy absorption and elasticity.  We will be discussing various popular methods of achieving 
  these factors in running shoes.

Analysis and discussion

  
Scientific background:
  
The running industry goes hand in hand with physical movement and the science behind it. The repeated interaction with the foot and the ground 
can be explained with newtons laws of motions. Newtons third law in this context states that when a runner exerts a force onto the ground, the 
ground will exert an equal and opposite reaction force on the foot. This plays a large role in running biomechanics (Matijevich et al. 2022).
The forces magnitude depends on factors such as the speed of running, foot strike and the construction of the full shoe. Energy store formulas 
are also considered when discussing running physics through the equations kinetic, elastic potential energy, both forces on the ground and body 
experiences their forces depending on their masses and construction, understanding this will allow us to know why shoe cushioning and energy 
transfers are engineered to cater for both the body and shoe allowing for an improvement in overall performance.

Comfort and performance:
  
Due to the repeated motion of the foot pressing on the ground causing reaction forces, energy is transferred continuously, we need to understand 
energy stores to know where this energy is displaced and returned (shorten,M.R. (2024). With the use of the midsole when it compresses, mechanical
energy is stored within the material as well as some dissipated through heat. Using materials that have elastic properties which allow for energy 
return is extremely helpful in ensuring energy is kept within the footwear so the runner does not need to work harder. This can be noticed through 
the bounce in materials such as foams and carbon plates which we will be further discussing.
  
Energy is also involved in the comfort of the sole of the shoe, when the midsole compresses on every push on the ground energy is absorbed so that 
the reaction force from the ground onto the lower limbs of the runner are not extreme enough to cause injury which could put them in a disadvantage 
in running. Through my research with (sun et al. 2020), softer midsoles with more energy absorption make running safer through cushioning so that the
runner is safe. the shape of the midsole is also very important, most models now stack foam at the heel as this is where runners first hit the ground 
meaning most energy is felt here so the return in energy can be maximised In running. Thicker midsoles and various densities can help in comfort but 
then lack in performance depending on the force exerted by the runner.
  
Design and manufacturing improvements:
  
Carbon fibre plating:
  
This technology embeds a carbon plate within the midsole to limit the bending in the foot as it hits the ground. Due to the stiffness in the carbon
fibre, the shoe stabilises the runner on the ground and also reduces energy loss as it can be used by the plate to push of the ground through the
return in bending it back into shape. Carbon fibre plating also is extremely light and comes in at 10-20g per shoe (sports coaching. 2025) which makes 
the return on energy per kg extremely efficient. 
  
Advanced foam materials:
Midsoles have also developed very efficient energy return materials which also help in comfort. Common materials include EVA, TPU AND PEBA (Rodrigo-
carranza et al. 2018) which all differ in densities and durability’s meaning they all perform better than each other in different aspects in racing. 
Many brands like adidas and Nike have invented their own technologies like boost and zoomX which hold various achievements in improving performance 
whilst maintaining factors like stability, weight and durability. 
  
Upper materials:

this makes up half of the shoe, most modern constructions use mesh or knitted synthetic materials as they allow for breathability to allow the runner 
to cool down and not hold sweat buildup in the shoe which can weigh them down. Nylon and polyester builds found through (Cordero-Sanchez et al. 2020) 
allow for lightweight and flexible bodies meaning the foot can move around freely and spread out for stability. The shoe will also be built smoothly 
so wind can flow around the foots pointed shape minimising resistance.

Limitations in future developments:
  
These technologies come with limitations to runners as well as the producers of models. Manufacturing these models have high costs due to the advanced
materials and research. This causes the retail value of these shoes to exceed £300 making them less accessible to customers. Many also state the durability
in these models lack structure such as in foam wearing out and tearing in uppers. There is also a divide in professional and daily running rising the 
debate in if these models are fitted for all runners and where do you call the line in who they are for. Performance models are seen by many as cheating 
and put you at an advantage as you can afford them, certain competitions put limits on foam stacking and weight on the shoes so they are more fair for all 
runners.
