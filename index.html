Abstract

  This project investigates how advancements in engineering and material sciences have influenced and improved the designing and work 
  behind modern running shoe to benefit in performances. The three technological methods being; carbon fibre plates, advanced midsole 
  foam and smart materials in the upper are common advanced implementations into models. Where research has been done on each individual 
  component in detail on how they contribute to lowering energy costs through energy stores and transfers. Investigations on the Nike 
  Vapourfly 4% have been used to provide evidence for the methods and how they have impacted performance. Disadvantages of this development
  include material costs and durability concerns where sky high retail prices cannot justify the product for the customer. Throughout 
  innovation and development, the modern running shoe has proved to be developed though must be balanced with sustainability.

Introduction

  Running shoes hold a historic reputation in its constant development and performance. Over successive decades with advancements 
  in technology and material design we have seen running shoes originate from  leather sole to 3d printed, carbon-plate models. 
  Through the growth of competitive running, the manufacturing market for running shoes has had to closely adapt to stay ahead 
  of the game with competitors. Traditional running attire would be using synthetic textiles on the body of the shoe complete with
  spikes set whereas now we are seeing new technologies being implemented such as carbon plating. These rely on three physical principles 
  within material science being; impact force, energy absorption and elasticity.  We will be discussing various popular methods of achieving 
  these factors in running shoes.

Analysis and discussion

  
  Scientific background:
  
  The running industry goes hand in hand with physical movement and the science. The repeated interaction with the foot and ground can be explained 
  with newtons laws of motions. Newtons third law states that when a runner exerts a force onto the ground, the ground will exert an equal and opposite 
  reaction force on the foot. This plays a large role in running biomechanics (Matijevich et al. 2022).The magnitude depends on factors such as the speed 
  of running, foot strike and the shoe construction. Energy store formulas are also considered when discussing running physics, both forces depend on thier 
  masses (F=ma), understanding this will allow us to know why shoe cushioning and energy transfers are engineered to cater for both the body and shoe therefor 
  an improvement in overall performance.

  Comfort and performance:
  
  When the foot presses on the ground, energy is transferred continuously, where it is dispaced and stored (shorten,M.R. (2024). When the midsole compresses, 
  mechanical energy is stored within the material as well as some dissipated through heat. Using materials that have elastic properties which allow for energy 
  return is extremely helpful in ensuring energy is maintained so the runner does not need to work harder. This can be noticed through the bounce in materials 
  such as foams and carbon plates which we will be further discussing.
  
  Energy is also involved in comfort, when the midsole compresses energy is absorbed so that the reaction force from the ground onto the runner are
  not extreme enough to cause injury which could put them at disadvantage. Through my research (sun et al. 2020), softer midsoles with more energy absorption make 
  running safer. the shape of the midsole is also very important, most models now stack foam at the heel as this is where runners hit the ground meaning most energy is 
  felt here so the return in energy can be maximised. Thicker midsoles can help in comfort but then lack in performance depending on the force exerted by the runner.
  
  Manufacturing improvements:
  
  Carbon fibre plating:
  
  This technology embeds a carbon plate within the midsole to limit the bending in the foot as it lands. Due to the stiffness in the plate, the shoe stabilises the runner 
  on the ground and also reduces energy loss as it can be used by the plate to push of the ground through bending back(elasticity) . Carbon fibre plating also is extremely light
  and comes in at 10-20g per shoe (sports coaching. 2025) which makes the return on energy per kilogram extremely efficient. 
  
  Advanced foam materials:
  
  Midsoles have also developed very efficient energy return systems in materials. Common materials include EVA, TPU AND PEBA (Rodrigo-
  carranza et al. 2018) which all differ in densities and durability’s meaning they all perform better comparing in different aspects in racing. 
  Many brands like adidas and Nike have invented their own technologies like boost and zoomX which hold various achievements in improving performance 
  whilst maintaining factors like stability, weight and durability. 
  
  Upper materials:
  
  This makes up half of the shoe, most constructions use mesh or knitted materials as they allow for breathability so the runner can cool down and not 
  hold sweat buildup in the shoe that can increase weight. Nylon and polyester builds found through (Cordero-Sanchez et al. 2020) allow for lightweight 
  and flexible bodies meaning the foot can move around freely and spread out for stability. The shape will also be built knowing  wind can flow around 
  the foots pointed shape minimising resistance.
    
  Evidence:
  
  With reference to the study: (hoogkamer, W. 2018) where they tested 16 male runners in the shoe model Nike vapour fly 4% compared to various running
  models. The Nike vapour fly incorporated the three discussed technologies from above. Runners ran 30 minutes readings at various speeds where results
  showed the new Nike model to have reduced energy costs by 4 percent compared to the other controlled models. This came in at 16.45 W/kg which can be seen 
  in the graph below. The results is what inspired the name of the 2017 performance running shoe.
    
  Limitations:
  
  These technologies come with limitations to runners and producers of models. advanced materials cause high manufaturing costs which cause the retail value 
  of these shoes to exceed £300 making them less accessible to customers. The durability in these models lack strength such as in foam wearing out and tearing
  in uppers. There is also a divide in professional and daily running rising the debate to where do you call the line in who they are for. Performance models 
  are seen by many as cheating and put you at an advantage, competitions put limits on foam stacking and weight on the shoes so they are more fair for all 
  runners.

  Conclusion:

  In conclusion we can state that advancements by engineering in running shoes have improved performance and ability through the discussed evolving technologies. 
  These methods combine to give us results in advancements through reducing energy costs and ensuring runners maximise performance. However the downsides of these
  abilities include extraordinary manufacturing costs and longevity concerns in materials including as divides in the community. We can conclude running shoes provide 
  us with evident results in performance where it can be worked on through sustainable methods.
